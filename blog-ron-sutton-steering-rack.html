Testing Ron Sutton Race Technology's New Mustang Power Rack

Jordan Welch, Hit It Autosport. Oct 16, 2026

Ron Sutton Race Technology just released a new power steering rack for 1974–2004 Mustangs: Mustang II, Fox body and SN95, plus most aftermarket chassis that use a Mustang rack. Sweet Manufacturing builds it, and Ron Sutton Race Technology is the exclusive distributor.

We're putting it on several of our cars this season. This post covers what the rack is, what we want out of it, and how we're going to test it. Install notes and results come next.

What the rack is

  • Power rack with an external dual power cylinder, built for very wide tires and high g-loads without the steering binding up.
  • Your choice of ratio, from the stock Mustang 1-7/8" up to 4-1/2" of rack travel per steering wheel turn.
  • Your choice of torsion bar, from .185" to .300", which sets how the steering feels in your hands.
  • Standard center-eye or 1" slotted-eye rack ends, with offset bushings for 15.50" and 16.00" mounting widths and 12mm, 14mm or 5/8" hardware.
  • Introductory price when we wrote this: $1,799 with centered ends or $1,899 with slotted ends, any ratio and any torsion bar.

Why we're swapping racks

Our cars run wide, sticky tires. The '96 autocross car is on 315/30/18 200-treadwear tires. That much rubber grips great on course, but it makes parking-lot and pit steering a workout, especially at walking speed when the tire is scrubbing instead of rolling.

The other problem shows up on course. Tight autocross elements, like a slow slalom or a hairpin into a box, ask for a lot of steering wheel in a short time. With the stock ratio you end up shuffling hands or crossing your arms right when you need to be smooth.

What we're hoping to see

  • Easier low-speed steering on 315s. Moving the car around the paddock and grid without wrestling it.
  • A quicker ratio for tight elements. Less wheel input through slaloms, hairpins and tight transitions, so our hands stay put more often.
  • No new bad habits. Steering that still feels calm at speed, without being twitchy on the straights or kicking back over bumps.

Why this rack might get us both

Those two goals usually fight each other. Speed up a manual rack and it gets heavier, because you lose leverage. That's why so many quick manual racks are miserable to park.

This rack separates the two. The ratio decides how quick the steering is. The torsion bar decides how much effort it takes before the power assist kicks in, which is what you feel through the wheel. In theory we can pick a quick ratio and still dial in the effort we want. Testing will tell us if it works that way on our cars.

How to read the ratios: they're listed as inches of rack travel per steering wheel turn, so a bigger number is quicker. Stock Mustang is 1-7/8". Ron suggests 2.50" for autocross and track cars, about a third quicker than stock, and 3.00" for autocross-only cars, about 60% quicker.

Which cars get it

We're running the rack across our Fox and SN95 builds, including the 98 Autocross Mustang, the 93 Drift Mustang and our '96 autocross car. Different power, weight and tires on each one will tell us a lot more than a single install would. We'll list the ratio and torsion bar we picked for each car in the install write-up.
